<p>Sam, a friend, sent me some of his poetry last week. I particularly enjoy this poem:</p>

<div class="quote">
The War Bride

<p>I lay awake last night and dreamed of her<br>
One heart still beating by the lonely Somme<br>
She wears these scars to remember the battles<br>
Only those she lost, never those she won.</p>

<p>Her hair is black like a murder of crows<br>
A liquor of ravens or a paper of pins<br>
Please wake her before her cover blows<br>
And her squadron of heroes comes running in.</p>

<p>Her father has made her wash for him<br>
Every night since her coming of age<br>
His vision soaks her body and his eyes<br>
Cut like a thousand obsidian blades</p>

<p>She sings him to his gentle sleep<br>
Selects one blade from the length of her arm<br>
She watches the water still and deep<br>
And leaves his body on the banks of the Marne.</p>

<p>I love her pale skin against my arm<br>
The thrust of her back and her desperate rage<br>
Her Passchendaele cross, her echo of Christ<br>
And her hundred thousand obsidian blades</p>

<p>My head on her shoulder like a murderer knows<br>
A disorder of conscience, a concubine's guilt<br>
I wake her, she rises soft and she goes<br>
To the house her holy soldier built
