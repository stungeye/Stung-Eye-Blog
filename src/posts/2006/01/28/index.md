>**It** is interesting to note that orthodox [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) believes that the language of the [Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas) is eternal and revealed in its wording and word order. Evidence for this belief is found in the [Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas) itself, where in the [Upanishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishads) they are described as the very "breath of God" (nihsvasitam brahma). 
>
>The [Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas) are therefore considered **"[the language of reality](http://www.google.ca/search?q=%22the+language+of+reality%22)"**, so to speak, and are *unauthored, even by God*, the [rishis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/rishis) or seers ascribed to them being merely individuals gifted with a special insight into reality with the power of perceiving these eternal sounds. At the beginning of every cycle of creation, *God himself "remembers"* the order of the Vedic words and propagates them through the rishis.
>
>It may be interesting to note that [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) encourages the Vedic mantras to be interpreted as liberally and as philosophically as possible.
