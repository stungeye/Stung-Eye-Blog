<p>My favorite letter in the <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_alphabet" target="_blank">Dutch alphabet</a> is 'ij', pronounced "eye". This letter -yes, two characters can be a single letter- puzzled me when I first arrived in Amsterdam. Locals would laugh when I pronounced mijn (English translation: my) with a 'j' sound in the middle.</p>

<p>Then one afternoon, I saw the word bedrijf (company) written in cursive.</p>

<p>Try this yourself: Grab a pen/paper and write the word "bedrijf" in cursive. What does the 'ij' remind you of?</p>

<p>'ij' in cursive = ÿ (A y with a <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaeresis" target="_blank">diaeresis</a>.)</p>

<p>The Dutch alphabet doesn't contain a 'y'. I find it fascinating that they would represent the 'y' sound with this cursive pseudo-y.</p>

<p>Check the Wikipedia entry on the <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Y" target="_blank">Dutch Y</a>.</p>
